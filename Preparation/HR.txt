Describe a production incident you were involved in. What did you do during and after the incident?

In my current role, I’m part of the extended production support group, so I get pulled in whenever the L1/L2 support team cannot handle an issue or the impact is high. 
During an incident, my first step is to understand the problem clearly: I look at logs, monitoring dashboards, and recent deployments to identify the root symptoms. 
Then I quickly estimate which customers are affected, the severity, and the time sensitivity, 
so we can decide whether to apply a quick workaround, roll back, or put the system into a safe but degraded mode.

If there is a known temporary fix or configuration change that can restore service quickly, 
I help implement that first to reduce impact, even if it means temporarily disabling the specific feature causing the issue. 
Once the system is stable, I work on the permanent fix—this might involve code changes, data correction scripts, or replaying/retriggering failed jobs where possible, 
and for cases that cannot be retriggered, planning compensating actions.

Tell me about a time you mentored or coached a junior engineer. What did you do specifically?

I started relationally—no "sir," just open dialogue. First, I gave them sandbox access (dev/demo environments) to explore freely, then had them document findings: 
"Why this stack? Pros/cons of our DB choices? Research alternatives." This built ownership. Next, we reviewed together—I shared real-world trade-offs (e.g., why we chose PostgreSQL over Mongo for our workloads), 
incorporated their ideas for process improvements (like better onboarding docs), and assigned bite-sized tasks: fix a small bug, add a simple API endpoint, contribute to a code review. 
Weekly 1:1s tracked progress with feedback loops.
